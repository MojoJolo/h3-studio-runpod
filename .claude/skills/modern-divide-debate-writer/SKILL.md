# Modern Divide Ragebait Debate Script Generator

## Purpose

Generate short-form English AI drama scripts for the Modern Divide brand.

The goal is not to teach a moral lesson or reveal a villain. The goal is to create a high-emotion, high-comment debate where viewers can genuinely defend either side.

The ideal viewer reaction is:

- "That first line is insane."
- "Wait, I actually understand why they said that."
- "No, the other person has a point too."
- "I need to read the comments."

The page is designed for an international audience.

---

## Core Brand Promise

Two sides. One argument. No easy answer.

Every script should create a disagreement between two people where:

1. The opening sounds provocative, unfair, extreme, or socially explosive.
2. The opposing person immediately challenges it.
3. The first person gives reasoning that makes the original position more understandable.
4. New context complicates the audience's initial judgment.
5. The final line is a sharp counterargument.
6. The conflict is NOT resolved.

The comments are where the verdict happens.

---

## Primary Viral Pattern

### TURN 1 — SHOCK

Open with the most provocative line immediately.

Examples:

- "I want a paternity test when the baby is born."
- "If we have a baby, I want thirty percent of your salary transferred to me."
- "Starting next month, I need you to pay rent."
- "I don't want you going on that trip."
- "Before the baby comes, I want the dog out of this house."

Do NOT begin with:

- "Can we talk?"
- "There's something I need to tell you."
- "I've been thinking."
- greetings
- setup dialogue
- exposition

The viewer should understand the conflict within the first 1–2 seconds.

### TURN 2 — OBVIOUS REACTION

The second character reacts exactly how the audience expects.

Example:

A: "I want a paternity test when the baby is born."

B: "So you think I cheated on you?"

This lets the audience emotionally commit to one side.

### TURN 3 — PRINCIPLE

The first character gives a short principle or practical reason rather than an excuse.

Example:

A: "No. I think every father should know for certain."

Good reasons include:

- fairness
- financial risk
- personal boundaries
- safety
- privacy
- equality
- responsibility
- tradition
- career consequences
- family loyalty
- previous experience

Avoid weak rationalizations that make the character obviously stupid or evil.

### TURN 4 — PERSONAL COST

The other character explains what that principle implies for them.

Example:

B: "You're asking me to prove I didn't betray you."

This turns an abstract argument into an emotional one.

### TURN 5 — HUMANIZING REVEAL

Reveal a personal experience, history, or consequence that suddenly makes the extreme position more understandable.

The reveal should NOT prove that A is correct. It should only make A understandable.

Useful reveals:

- parent experienced betrayal
- family lost money
- career was derailed
- sibling was injured
- parent became financially dependent
- previous partner broke trust
- childhood experience shaped the belief
- company/family previously took advantage of them

Avoid soap-opera twists such as:

- secret billionaire
- hidden inheritance
- surprise CEO
- fake identity
- instant karma
- revenge reveal

Modern Divide is debate, not morality fantasy.

### TURN 6 — COUNTERPUNCH

End with the strongest line in the script.

The final line should attack the logic of the previous reveal without dismissing the emotion behind it.

Examples of the pattern:

- "And now I'm paying for what your mother did?"
- "Why should I risk my future just because I'm the mother?"
- "And now he gets punished for what somebody else's dog did?"
- "You chose to have children. You don't get priority over my life because I didn't."
- "If you need access to trust me, you already don't."

Then:

HARD CUT.

No reply.
No apology.
No agreement.
No reconciliation.
No moral.
No narrator.

---

## Script Length

Default:

24 seconds total

3 clips × approximately 8 seconds

Each clip should feel dense.

Target approximately:

- 2–4 spoken lines per clip
- 5–8 total exchanges across the reel
- a new piece of information every 2–4 seconds

Avoid long reaction pauses.

Reaction beats should normally be very short unless silence itself is the punchline.

The pacing should feel like an argument already in progress.

---

## Dialogue Style

Use:

- natural conversational English
- neutral international phrasing
- short sentences
- emotionally sharp language
- restrained but tense delivery
- lines that sound quotable in comments

Avoid:

- speeches
- therapy language
- academic wording
- overly polished dialogue
- melodrama
- constant shouting
- obvious exposition
- characters explaining facts they both already know unless the audience needs the information

Prefer:

> "You want a modern marriage with a traditional husband?"

Over:

> "I feel that your expectations reflect an inconsistent application of traditional gender roles."

---

## Tone

The ideal tone is:

absurd but realistic

The premise may sound extreme at first, but it must be something a real person could plausibly say.

The audience should think:

> "That's insane."

Then, a few seconds later:

> "Okay… I kind of understand why."

Do not make the argument so absurd that one side becomes indefensible.

## Two-Sided Debate Rule

Before approving a script, answer:

Can a reasonable person defend Character A?

Can a reasonable person defend Character B?

If either answer is no, rewrite.

Do not create:

- obvious abusers vs innocent victims
- blatant cheaters vs loyal partners
- cartoonishly cruel parents
- evil bosses with no defensible position
- characters demanding obviously illegal or dangerous behavior

High emotion is good.

One-sided morality is not.

---

## Character Rules

Default:

- exactly two visible adult characters
- both understand what they are arguing about
- both believe they are being reasonable
- neither character is visually coded as the villain
- no sneering
- no evil smirks
- no theatrical crying
- no cartoon rage unless the scene truly requires it

The disagreement should feel like two real adults whose values conflict.

---

## Visual / Production Rules

Unless the user says otherwise:

- exactly two visible characters
- no narrator
- no voice-over
- no on-screen text
- no subtitles or burned-in captions
- natural restrained acting
- realistic contemporary locations
- neutral international English
- no non-diegetic music
- characters face each other, not the camera
- hard cut on unresolved tension

Good settings:

- bedroom
- kitchen
- living room
- office
- café
- car
- hotel room
- restaurant
- family home

Keep production simple enough for AI video generation.

---

## Preferred 3-Clip Structure

**CLIP 1 — THE EXPLOSIVE PREMISE**

Start immediately with the controversial demand or statement.

Then give the obvious reaction.

Goal: Stop the scroll.

**CLIP 2 — MAKE THE "BAD" SIDE SOUND REASONABLE**

Give the extreme character a principle, logic, or context.

Then let the other character explain why that reasoning is unfair.

Goal: Flip the viewer.

**CLIP 3 — PERSONAL REVEAL + COUNTERPUNCH**

Introduce personal history or a real consequence.

Then end with the other character's strongest response.

Goal: Make the audience argue after the video ends.

B:

────────

Do NOT add on-screen end text.

Do NOT explain which side is right.

A short explanation after the script is optional, but the script itself is the primary output.

## Dialogue Density Check

Bad:

A: "I want a paternity test."

[3-second stare]

B: "Why?"

[2-second pause]

Too slow.

Better:

A: "I want a paternity test when the baby is born."

B: "So you think I cheated on you?"

A: "No. I think every father should know for certain."

The viewer should receive constant new information.

---

## Anti-Patterns

Reject or rewrite scripts that rely mainly on:

- secret rich person reveals
- instant karma
- revenge fantasy
- "then everyone clapped"
- a narrator explaining the lesson
- obvious moral winners
- long setup
- slow pacing
- generic relationship complaints
- repetitive cheating plots
- one character behaving stupidly only to make the other look smart
- final reconciliation
- obvious lecture about feminism, masculinity, parenting, etc.

Characters should embody perspectives.

They should not sound like social-media essays.

---

## Quality Gate

Before outputting, silently verify:

1. Hook — Would the first line stop someone scrolling?
2. Rage — Does the topic create an immediate emotional reaction?
3. Realism — Could actual people plausibly have this argument?
4. Side A — Can reasonable viewers defend A?
5. Side B — Can reasonable viewers defend B?
6. Escalation — Does each clip introduce new information?
7. Flip — Does the audience's judgment become less certain by Clip 2 or 3?
8. Final Line — Is the final line memorable enough to quote in comments?
9. Unresolved — Does the video end before the argument is settled?
10. Production — Can it be generated cleanly with exactly two visible characters in three 8-second clips?

If the script fails 2 or more checks, rewrite it before outputting.

## Topic Generation Method

When asked for "another one," do NOT merely change names.

Choose a different conflict by combining:

VALUE A vs VALUE B

Examples:

- equality vs practicality
- privacy vs transparency
- loyalty vs boundaries
- safety vs fairness
- tradition vs autonomy
- career vs family
- ownership vs partnership
- parents vs child-free adults
- personal responsibility vs social responsibility
- financial contribution vs domestic contribution
- emotional history vs present-day fairness

Then invent an extreme but plausible demand that exposes the conflict.

Useful formulas:

"If X happens, I want Y."

"I'm not doing X unless Y."

"Starting now, Y changes."

These produce stronger hooks than generic arguments.

The best Modern Divide script makes the audience angry because both sides have enough of a point to fight over.

## H3 production notes (learned in this project)

These come from real renders in this repo and apply whenever a Modern Divide script is turned into MiniMax H3 prompts:

- **Already in position.** Clip 1's first visual beat has both characters already facing each other mid-argument. Never open with someone walking in or sitting down: a walking opening cost real retention on a posted episode.
- **Make the two characters obviously different** (different gender, or clearly different race/skin tone and age), not two similar people told apart only by hair or wardrobe color. The model otherwise swaps who's speaking.
- **Speaker lock every clip.** Name who speaks each line by visible description, and keep the non-speaker's lips closed while the other talks.
- **Deny extras and captions explicitly.** "Exactly two people, no one else in frame, no silhouettes or reflections of a third person" and "no on-screen subtitles, captions or text of any kind". Renders in this project have added a phantom third person and burned-in captions when this was left implicit.
- **Dense dialogue fits.** 2–4 lines per 8-second clip is fine for H3 if each line is short. Time-stamp each line inside the clip.
