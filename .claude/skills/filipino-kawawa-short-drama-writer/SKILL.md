# Filipino Kawawa / Everyday Struggle Drama for MiniMax H3

Created 2026-10-08 as a separate brand from Tagalog ragebait. **Unproven format:** treat it
as a test and keep the hook discipline strict.

Format: 24 s · 3 clips × 8 s · exactly 2 visible adults · conflict or pain at 0:00.

Desired viewer reaction:

> *"Ako 'to."* / *"Ang sakit."* → tags their mom, partner or sibling → shares.

Pure sadness gets scrolled past. What holds viewers is **a painful fact they recognize, then a
reveal that makes it hurt more.**

---

# 0. WHAT THIS SKILL OWNS, AND WHAT IT BORROWS

This skill owns the **story**: modes, hook, clip structure, ending, acting tone.

For everything **technical**, read and follow `filipino-ragebait-short-drama-writer` and apply
these sections exactly as written there:

- §10 Filipino dialogue style · §11 grammar preservation · §12 "ako" rule
- §13 Filipino accent block · §14–16 pronunciation locks **including the USER-VERIFIED WORD
  LIST** (Nánay = NA-nay, A-te never "eight", sábong = SA-bong, …) · §17 "Oo" rule
- §18 H3 audio safety · §19 exact dialogue lock · §20 character identity lock
- §21 speaker lock · §22 visual rules + LOCATION LOCK · §23 camera rules
- §25 H3 prompt format (including the Auto-mode plain-text exception) · §26 don't shorten later clips

Do **NOT** borrow its story rules (§1–9, §30): no "final line makes the antagonist worse", no
"choose the outrageous", no shameless escalator. Those are ragebait. They break kawawa.

# 1. TWO MODES

| | **A. Everyday struggle** (default, ~85%) | **B. Hurt by someone** (rare, ~15%) |
|---|---|---|
| Who | someone struggling + someone who loves them | victim + someone careless toward them |
| Villain | **none**: the problem is life (money, school, work, health, distance) | ungrateful child, embarrassed relative, dismissive in-law |
| Hook | the painful fact, said plainly at 0:00 | a hurtful line said **to** the victim at 0:00 |
| Final line | a quiet reveal of what the other person has silently been doing or giving up | the victim's quiet reveal of how much they gave up |
| Viewer reaction | relates, tags family, comments "laban lang" | pity + anger at the careless one |

Mode B's careless person is **not a ragebait antagonist**: they are tired, embarrassed or
thoughtless, not shameless. Keep them human.

### Filipino-in-America (both modes)

A big share of topics are Filipinos in the US (nurses, caregivers, two jobs, rent, missing home,
missed funerals and weddings, family thinking America = rich). Staging rule: **both people are in
the same room**. H3 cannot do video calls. Use one of:

- two family members who both live in the US (sisters, couple, coworkers) in a small US apartment
- a parent visiting the US
- a balikbayan home visit in the Philippines

Show the US through set dressing (small apartment, scrubs, a US-style kitchen, snow outside the
window), never through exposition.

---

# 2. HOOK RULE: THE PAINFUL FACT AT 0:00

The first spoken line states the wound plainly. The viewer must feel it in **~2 seconds**.

Do NOT open with: greetings, "May sasabihin ako", "Kailangan nating mag-usap", "Okay ka lang?",
sighing silence, someone walking in, or a vague mood.

Hook patterns (patterns, not lines to copy):

- **The count:** a number that shows how long it has hurt. *"Pang-limampung reject na 'to."*
- **The confession:** something they've been hiding. *"Tatlong buwan na akong nagkukunwaring pumapasok."*
- **The verdict:** the result that just landed. *"Bagsak ako."* · *"Negative na naman."*
- **The impossible math:** money that doesn't add up. *"Kulang na naman tayo ng tatlong libo."*
- **The distance:** what being far away cost. *"Hindi ako nakauwi sa libing ni Papa."*
- **Mode B, the hurtful ask:** *"Huwag ka na munang pumunta. Nakakahiya sa in-laws ko."*

**First-line test:** if I heard ONLY this sentence while scrolling, would I feel something? If
not, rewrite it.

---

# 3. CLIP STRUCTURE

Every clip must reveal something NEW. Comfort alone is not new information.

- **Clip 1, THE WOUND:** the painful fact + the other person's first short response.
- **Clip 2, IT'S DEEPER:** the real size of it: how long, what's at stake, what they've been
  hiding, why it matters more than it first sounded.
- **Clip 3, THE QUIET REVEAL:** the final line reframes everything. Usually the comforter's own
  hidden sacrifice (*"Alam ko, anak. Kaya lagi kitang binabaunan."*), or the struggler's smallest,
  most specific detail (*"Itong sapatos na suot ko pa rin."*). Hard cut on it.

### Ending rules

- **Comfort is never the ending.** *"Okay lang 'yan, anak."* / *"Kaya natin 'to."* / a hug as the
  last beat = dead video. If a comforting line appears, the final line must undercut it.
- The final line is **specific and concrete** (a pair of shoes, a ring, a lunch skipped, a
  tarpaulin already printed), not a feeling.
- Cut **before** the reaction. No hug, no crying response, no "salamat". The viewer finishes the
  scene in their head.
- No moral lesson, no narrator, no "laban lang" slogan said aloud.

### Pacing

- 3–4 short lines per clip, 3–10 words each. Pauses are allowed but short (max ~1 s).
- Clip 3's final line gets room: ~2 s, delivered slowly.

---

# 4. CHARACTERS AND ACTING

- Exactly **two visible adults**. Children, sick parents, absent family, bosses: **OFF-SCREEN**,
  only mentioned. State this explicitly in every clip.
- Make the two visually distinct (age gap, gender, clothing) so H3 doesn't swap speakers.
- **Restrained, real acting.** Wet eyes, a voice that breaks on one word, a long look down, a
  hand that stops mid-task. NO wailing, NO teleserye sobbing, NO dramatic collapse.
- The comforter is calm and tender. The final line is said **softly and simply**, never as a
  dramatic announcement.
- Hands doing something ordinary (folding laundry, counting bills, peeling garlic, packing a
  baon) makes it feel real.

# 5. VISUALS AND SOUND

- Photorealistic, contemporary. Modest Filipino home (or small US apartment for US topics).
  Warm, natural, slightly dim light: late afternoon or a single lamp at night.
- Static or very slow push-in two-shot. Same LOCATION LOCK rules as the Tagalog skill.
- overall_soundscape: quiet and specific (electric fan, rain on the roof, distant tricycle,
  apartment heater, kitchen sounds).
- non_diegetic_music: **N/A** by default. Music competes with the dialogue in H3. Only add
  very soft, very low piano if explicitly asked.

---

# 6. WORKFLOW

1. Pick the mode (A unless the topic says otherwise).
2. Write the hook. Run the first-line test.
3. Plan clip 2's "it's deeper" reveal.
4. Write the quiet final line. Check it's concrete and not comfort.
5. Audit the dialogue with the Tagalog skill's §10–12 and §17 (pronouns, ako, Oo).
6. Add pronunciation locks from its word list (3–8 per clip, only risky words).
7. Build the 3 H3 prompts with ALL the borrowed technical blocks repeated in every clip.

# 7. SELF-CRITIQUE BEFORE OUTPUT

- **Hook:** does the first line state the painful fact within ~2 s?
- **New information:** does every clip reveal something new? Is clip 2 more than comfort?
- **Ending:** is the final line a concrete reveal? Does it make it hurt more? Is there NO hug,
  NO comfort, NO lesson after it?
- **Realism:** would a Filipino family recognize this from their own life?
- **Mode B check:** is the careless person human, not a cartoon villain?
- **Grammar and pronunciation:** borrowed rules applied (ako, Oo, Nánay, A-te, word list)?
- **Identity and speakers:** two distinct adults, locked sides, S1/S2 locked, final speaker named?
- **Visuals:** exactly two people visible, everyone else explicitly OFF-SCREEN, location locked?

If any answer is NO, revise before output.

# 8. AVOID

Ragebait escalation · shameless antagonists · shouting matches · comfort or hug endings ·
moral lessons · narrator · melodramatic sobbing · vague feelings instead of concrete details ·
video calls or phone screens · visible children · third visible person · loud music.
