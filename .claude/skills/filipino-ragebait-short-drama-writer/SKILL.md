# Filipino Ragebait Drama for MiniMax H3

## Purpose

Generate short-form Filipino / Taglish ragebait drama scripts optimized for MiniMax H3 video generation.

Primary format:
- 24 seconds total
- 3 clips × 8 seconds
- exactly 2 visible adult characters per clip
- conflict begins immediately
- every clip escalates
- final line should usually make the situation WORSE

Primary target:
Facebook Reels / YouTube Shorts style Filipino drama with high comment potential.

Desired audience reaction:

"Ang kapal ng mukha... pero may mga taong ganyan talaga."

The tone is:

ABSURD-BUT-PLAUSIBLE.

Do not make the situation so unrealistic that it becomes parody.

Do not make it too reasonable or emotionally soft.

The best scripts feel outrageous while still being imaginable in a real Filipino:
- family
- relationship
- workplace
- neighborhood
- wedding
- money dispute
- social situation

---

# 1. CORE STORY ENGINE

Default structure:

OUTRAGEOUS HOOK
→ SHORT REACTION
→ SHAMELESS EXPLANATION
→ WORSE REVEAL
→ STRONG REACTION
→ FINAL LINE THAT MAKES THE ANTAGONIST WORSE

Example:

Mother:
"Anak, kailangan ng kuya mo ng fifty thousand. May utang na naman siya sa sugal."

Daughter:
"Ma, bakit ako magbabayad ng sugal niya?"

Mother:
"Eh sino pa?"

Then escalate:

Daughter:
"Last month binayaran ko na yung sugal niya!"

Mother:
"Hindi naman niya kasalanang natalo siya."

Then escalate AGAIN:

Mother:
"Kulang 'yon."

Daughter:
"Bakit kulang?"

Mother:
"Kailangan pa niya ng pangbawi."

The final line should ideally create a stronger reaction than the opening premise.

---

# 2. HOOK RULE

The FIRST SPOKEN LINE is the most important line.

Conflict must already exist at 0:00.

Do NOT waste the first seconds on:
- entrances
- greetings
- walking
- staring around
- discovering an object
- "May sasabihin ako"
- "Kailangan nating mag-usap"
- slow exposition
- establishing shots

If an object matters, it should already be visible in frame 1.

GOOD HOOKS:

"Kambal anak natin, bakit di ako ang tatay ng isa?"

"Buntis ka? Akala ko ba tomboy ka?"

"Anak, kailangan ng kuya mo ng fifty thousand. May utang na naman siya sa sugal."

"Umalis ka bago mag-six. Darating asawa ko."

The first line should often reveal 60–80% of the scandal.

Do not hide the premise just to preserve a twist.

The twist should make an already-interesting premise WORSE.

---

# 3. PACING

Each 8-second clip should contain approximately 3–4 short dialogue beats.

Prefer:
- 3–10 words per line
- immediate responses
- blunt accusations
- short questions
- short reactions
- quotable lines

Do not write speeches.

The viewer should understand the conflict within the first 2–3 seconds.

Typical structure:

CLIP 1:
Reveal the scandal.

CLIP 2:
Reveal WHO / WHY / WHAT actually happened.

CLIP 3:
Reveal the shameless justification, worse implication, or hidden consequence.

Do not repeat information unless repetition creates stronger emotional impact.

---

# 4. ESCALATION RULE

Every clip must introduce NEW information.

Clip 2 cannot merely argue about Clip 1.

Clip 3 cannot merely continue shouting.

Each clip should materially worsen the story.

GOOD:

Clip 1:
One twin is not the husband's biological child.

Clip 2:
The other father is the husband's brother.

Clip 3:
Wife minimizes it:

"At least magkapatid naman yung mga tatay."

BAD:

"You cheated!"

"I'm sorry."

"How could you?"

"It was a mistake."

That is emotionally repetitive.

---

# 5. ANTAGONIST BEHAVIOR

The strongest ragebait antagonists do NOT behave like obvious cartoon villains.

They usually:
- speak calmly
- believe their logic is reasonable
- minimize what they did
- redirect blame
- justify selfishness as practicality
- use family obligation
- become offended when challenged
- double down after being caught
- rarely apologize

Good antagonist logic:

"Eh malaki naman sweldo mo."

"Kapatid mo 'yon. Pababayaan mo?"

"Hindi naman niya kasalanang natalo siya."

"At least magkapatid naman yung mga tatay."

"Hindi naman niya kailangang malaman."

"Kailangan pa niya ng pangbawi."

The antagonist's calm sincerity often makes the line more infuriating.

---

# 6. AUDIENCE-SURROGATE CHARACTER

The other character represents the viewer.

Their dialogue should usually be short.

Examples:

"Ha?"

"Ano?"

"Rin?"

"Bakit kulang?"

"Ex ng girlfriend mo?!"

"What the fuck?"

"Gago ka!"

Their purpose is to:
- expose the absurdity
- ask what the audience is thinking
- clarify relationships
- set up the antagonist's next terrible answer

Do not give them long moral speeches.

# 7. TOPIC GENERATION

Rotate outrage sources.

Do NOT repeatedly generate only husband-wife cheating stories.

Strong categories include:

- pregnancy
- paternity
- twins
- siblings
- mother / daughter
- father / child
- boyfriend / girlfriend
- lesbian / gay relationships
- ex-partners
- in-laws
- family favoritism
- lazy relatives
- breadwinner abuse
- gambling
- loans
- debts
- inheritance
- OFW money
- weddings
- pregnancy favoritism
- parenting
- housing
- property ownership
- workplace entitlement
- salary inequality
- household labor
- status / class
- friend betrayal
- school / parent conflicts

Pregnancy works especially well when the reveal changes the assumed relationship.

Examples:

"Buntis ako. Pero hindi si Papa ang tatay."

"Dalawa sila, Ma."

"Yung ex ni Bea."

"Kuya mo."

"Kambal anak natin, bakit di ako ang tatay ng isa?"

Do not merely recycle the same relationship with renamed characters.

Change the emotional mechanism.

# 8. ABSURD-BUT-PLAUSIBLE TEST

Before accepting a concept, ask:

1. Could something roughly like this happen in real life?

2. Would people immediately argue about it in the comments?

If YES to both, the concept is probably strong.

Too normal:

"My husband doesn't help around the house."

Better:

Husband is unemployed.
Wife pays everything.
He refuses chores because:

"Asawa mo ako, hindi katulong."

Absurdity should come from HUMAN LOGIC.

Not impossible events.

---

# 9. ENDING RULE

The final line is critical.

Prefer a final line that:
- reveals worse information
- shows entitlement
- shows lack of remorse
- creates a new ethical problem
- reframes the whole conflict
- exposes terrible logic
- makes the audience immediately want to comment

Strong endings:

"Kailangan pa niya ng pangbawi."

"Hindi naman niya kailangang malaman."

"At least magkapatid naman yung mga tatay."

"Asawa mo ako, hindi katulong."

"Ikaw naman hindi buntis. Pwede ka maghintay."

Weak endings:

"Sorry."

"Please understand."

"I didn't mean it."

"We'll talk later."

"I love you."

Do not soften the ending.

CUT IMMEDIATELY after the strongest line.

---

# 10. FILIPINO DIALOGUE STYLE

Use natural contemporary Metro Manila Tagalog / Taglish.

Dialogue should sound spoken.

Not literary.

Prefer:

"Eh sino pa?"

"Anong Nico?"

"Nag-away kasi kami."

"Ma, bakit ako...?"

"Last month binayaran ko na..."

Avoid overly formal Filipino unless character or context requires it.

Do not force English into every line.

English is useful for short reactions:

"What the fuck?"

Do not overuse it.

---

# 11. TAGALOG GRAMMAR PRESERVATION

Pronouns and verb aspect are CRITICAL.

Never casually rewrite:

mo → ko

ko → mo

natin → namin

umalis → umaalis

umalis → aalis

The exact relationship between:
- speaker
- listener
- owner
- action
- tense

must remain correct.

Audit every line before output.

---

# 12. "AKO" RULE

NEVER shorten standalone:

"ako"

into:

"'ko"

BAD:

"Nag-resign na 'ko."

GOOD:

"Nag-resign na ako."

Normal grammatical "ko" remains "ko":

"trabaho ko"

"asawa ko"

"sinabi ko"

"pera ko"

Vernacular demonstratives are okay:

"'to"

"'yan"

"'yon"

Avoid:

"'kaw"

Prefer:

"ikaw"

Prefer full:

"hindi"

when pronunciation reliability matters.

---

# 13. FILIPINO ACCENT BLOCK

Every independently generated H3 clip MUST contain this block:

Both characters are native Metro Manila Filipino speakers.
They speak natural contemporary Tagalog/Taglish with authentic Metro Manila Filipino pronunciation.
Use clear Filipino vowels and natural Filipino rhythm.
Do NOT use an American, English, Spanish, or other foreign accent when speaking Tagalog.
Do NOT anglicize Filipino vowels.
Do NOT speak pronunciation instructions aloud.

Repeat this block in EVERY clip.

Do not assume pronunciation carries over from the previous generation.

---

# 14. PRONUNCIATION LOCKS

Only pronunciation-lock words that are likely to fail.

Do NOT accent every Filipino word.

Ideal:
3–8 locks per clip.

Format:

PRONUNCIATION LOCK:
- anák = a-NAK, stress on NAK
- tátay = TA-tay, stress on TA
- asáwa = a-SA-wa, stress on SA

Pronunciation instructions are SILENT.

They must NEVER be spoken aloud.

Stress marks may also appear directly inside dialogue.

Example:

<d>[Tagalog] Kambál anák natin...</d>

---

# 15. SPECIAL FILIPINO KINSHIP PRONUNCIATION RULES

These require EXTRA protection because H3 tends to anglicize them.

## NANAY

Correct target pronunciation (user-verified 2026-10-05; this replaces an earlier "naNAY" rule):

Nanay = NA-nay

Two syllables.

Stress on NA.

Use this silent pronunciation lock:

- Nánay = NA-nay, two syllables, stress on NA, Filipino vowels

If written with a stress marker in dialogue:

Nánay

Do NOT stress the second syllable (na-NAY).

Do NOT use an English-style pronunciation.

---

## ATE

H3 may pronounce:

Ate

like the English word:

"eight"

or:

"Eyte"

This is WRONG.

For reliability, write the spoken word as:

A-te

Example:

<d>[Tagalog] Kay A-te na muna yung venue.</d>

Use this lock:

- A-te = AH-teh, spoken SLOWLY as two clearly separated syllables with a tiny break (A... te), Filipino vowels, NEVER a quick one-syllable "ate", NEVER "eight" or "Eyte"

User-verified (2026-10-03): H3 still rushed "A-te" into one quick syllable with the shorter lock above, so always use this slow, separated wording.

The first syllable is an open Filipino "A" sound.

The second syllable is:

"te"

Do NOT pronounce it as one English syllable.

Default rule:

When "Ate" is spoken aloud in an H3 prompt, prefer writing:

A-te

unless testing proves plain "Ate" is reliable.

# 17. "OO" RULE

Bare:

Oo

may be pronounced like English:

"Oh."

Prefer:

O-o

Or naturally rewrite as:

"Sige."

"Tama."

"Ganun na nga."

Avoid bare "Oo" when pronunciation quality matters.

# 18. H3 AUDIO SAFETY

H3 sometimes speaks stage directions.

Every clip MUST contain:

CRITICAL AUDIO RULE:
There is NO narrator.
There is NO voice-over.
ONLY dialogue written inside <d>...</d> may be spoken aloud.
Everything outside <d>...</d> is SILENT visual direction only.
Never speak timestamps, character descriptions, actions, camera instructions, speaker labels, pronunciation guidance, or production notes.

Every spoken line must use:

<d>[Tagalog] ...</d>

or:

<d>[English] ...</d>

---

# 19. EXACT DIALOGUE LOCK

Every clip MUST contain:

CRITICAL DIALOGUE PRESERVATION:
Speak every <d> line EXACTLY as written.
Preserve all Tagalog pronouns and verb forms exactly.
Do NOT replace "mo" with "ko" or "ko" with "mo."
Never shorten standalone "ako" into "'ko."
Normal grammatical "ko" remains "ko."
Do NOT paraphrase, normalize, or rewrite the dialogue.

This is especially important when story logic depends on:
- ownership
- family relationship
- who performed an action
- tense
- pronouns
- verb aspect

---

# 20. CHARACTER IDENTITY LOCK

MiniMax H3 may interchange characters.

Treat this as a MAJOR generation risk.

Every clip MUST independently define both characters.

Format:

CHARACTER 1 — NAME:
- age
- gender presentation
- skin tone
- hair
- clothing
- body / visual characteristics
- screen position
- speaker ID

CHARACTER 2 — NAME:
- age
- gender presentation
- skin tone
- hair
- clothing
- body / visual characteristics
- screen position
- speaker ID

Then add:

CRITICAL CHARACTER IDENTITY LOCK:

NEVER swap their faces.
NEVER swap their hairstyles.
NEVER swap their bodies.
NEVER swap their ages.
NEVER swap their wardrobe.
NEVER swap their screen positions.
NEVER swap their voices.
NEVER swap their dialogue.
NEVER morph one character into the other.

Default:

Character 1 = SCREEN-LEFT

Character 2 = SCREEN-RIGHT

They do NOT cross.

They do NOT exchange sides.

Maintain the same facial identity from first frame to last frame.

Repeat the full lock in EVERY independently generated clip.

Do not assume H3 remembers the previous clip.

---

# 21. SPEAKER LOCK

Every clip must explicitly assign:

Character 1 = S1

Character 2 = S2

At the end of every clip:

FINAL SPEAKER LOCK:
S1 = CHARACTER 1 ONLY.
S2 = CHARACTER 2 ONLY.
Never exchange voices or dialogue.
Only the active speaker moves their lips.
The listener's mouth remains naturally closed.

For a critical ending line, explicitly state who MUST say it.

Example:

The person saying:

"At least magkapatid naman yung mga tátay."

MUST be MIA.

Then restate:
- appearance
- screen side
- speaker ID

This reduces speaker reversal.

---

# 22. VISUAL RULES

Default visual style:

- photorealistic
- contemporary Philippines
- Metro Manila unless location matters
- natural lighting
- natural restrained acting
- realistic micro-expressions
- no exaggerated teleserye acting
- no cartoon reactions
- no narrator
- no on-screen text
- no subtitles
- exactly 2 visible characters
- no unnecessary extras

Third characters may exist OFF-SCREEN.

Explicitly state:

Do NOT show another character.

If babies, children, siblings, lovers, parents, or coworkers are mentioned but should not appear:

explicitly state that they remain OFF-SCREEN.

---

## LOCATION LOCK (user feedback 2026-10-05)

In a test episode, the dining table disappeared in some clips and the room drifted between clips. Every clip MUST repeat a LOCATION LOCK block:

- list the fixed set pieces AND where they are (e.g. "small wooden dining table in the center with two monobloc chairs facing each other; electric fan in the back-left corner; window with sheer curtains on the right; religious statue on a shelf on the back wall")
- "Every camera angle shows this same room. Only the camera moves. Do not add, remove or relocate furniture or props between shots or clips. Same lighting and time of day throughout."
- if anything changes during the story (a broken glass, a dropped phone), it stays changed in later shots

Characters seated at a table stay seated at that same table in every clip.

# 23. CAMERA RULES

Keep camera simple.

Prefer:

- single continuous shot
- stable medium two-shot
- medium-close two-shot
- intimate two-shot

Avoid:

- rapid cuts
- elaborate blocking
- characters walking around
- crossing screen sides
- spins
- complicated camera moves
- complex physical interactions

Stable camera improves:
- identity consistency
- lip sync
- speaker assignment
- continuity

Characters should remain on fixed sides whenever possible.

---

# 24. ACTING RULE

The SCRIPT may be ridiculous.

The PERFORMANCE should not be ridiculous.

Use phrases such as:

"Natural restrained acting."

"Absurd situation played completely seriously."

"Do not exaggerate reactions."

"Character genuinely believes this logic is reasonable."

The antagonist's calm delivery is often funnier and more infuriating than shouting.

Example:

"Kailangan pa niya ng pangbawi."

should sound sincere.

Not like a punchline.

# 25. H3 PROMPT FORMAT

Default output:

ONE fenced code block.

Three prompts.

Each starts with:

8 | integrated_multimodal_description:

Separate clips using:

=====

Every clip must independently contain:

1. environment
2. exact character descriptions
3. screen positions
4. character identity lock
5. Filipino accent block
6. pronunciation locks
7. audio rule
8. dialogue preservation rule
9. timed dialogue
10. speaker lock
11. camera instruction
12. overall_soundscape
13. non_diegetic_music

End with:

overall_soundscape: ...

non_diegetic_music: N/A

Do NOT omit:

8 |

**Exception — H3 Studio Auto mode:** when the request says "no code fences" / "plain text only" (H3 Studio's Auto profile does), output the same three prompts as PLAIN TEXT with no fence at all. Auto's parser splits on the `=====` lines, and a code fence wrapped around all three clips makes it drop clips. Everything else in this section still applies.

---

# 26. DO NOT SHORTEN LATER CLIPS TOO MUCH

Never assume Clip 2 or Clip 3 can simply say:

"Same characters as before."

Each H3 clip may be generated independently.

Repeat:
- hair
- clothing
- age
- screen side
- speaker ID
- identity locks
- accent instructions
- pronunciation locks
- the LOCATION LOCK (furniture and props with their positions)

Continuity instructions must be redundant on purpose.

# 27. WORKFLOW FOR NEW SCRIPTS

When asked for a new script:

STEP 1:
Choose one emotionally provocative conflict from a proven pillar (§30.3), then run the
first-line, conflict (3 of 5) and retention gates in §30.7 before writing.

STEP 2:
Create the strongest possible FIRST spoken line.

STEP 3:
Make sure the premise is understandable immediately.

STEP 4:
Plan one meaningful escalation for Clip 2.

STEP 5:
Plan an even worse escalation for Clip 3.

STEP 6:
Find the shameless final line.

STEP 7:
Audit Filipino dialogue for:
- natural wording
- pronoun correctness
- speaker ownership
- verb aspect
- unnecessary exposition
- weak lines
- repeated information

STEP 8:
Identify risky pronunciation words.

STEP 9:
Apply known special cases:
- Nanay → Nánay (NA-nay)
- Ate → A-te, never "eight"
- Oo → O-o or rewrite
- standalone ako → never "'ko"

STEP 10:
Create full H3 prompt.

STEP 11:
Add strict character identity locks.

STEP 12:
Add strict speaker locks.

STEP 13:
Confirm exactly two visible characters.

STEP 14:
Check that every clip introduces new information.

# 28. SELF-CRITIQUE BEFORE OUTPUT

Before returning a script, verify:

## HOOK

Does the first spoken line immediately reveal the scandal?

Would a viewer understand the conflict in 2–3 seconds?

## RAGE

Is there at least one line likely to make viewers say:

"Ang kapal."

## ESCALATION

Does Clip 2 reveal something new?

Does Clip 3 make it worse?

## ENDING

Is the final line one of the strongest lines in the entire script?

## REALISM

Is the situation outrageous but still imaginable?

## DIALOGUE

Would a Filipino actually say these lines aloud?

## PACING

Can every clip realistically fit into 8 seconds?

## GRAMMAR

Are:
- mo
- ko
- natin
- namin
- ako
- verb aspects

correct?

## PRONUNCIATION

Are only risky words stress-locked?

If "Nanay" appears:
Is it locked as NA-nay (Nánay)?

If "Ate" appears:
Is spoken dialogue written as A-te when needed?
Is it explicitly forbidden from sounding like "eight"?

If "Oo" appears:
Should it be O-o instead?

## IDENTITY

Are the two characters visually distinct?

Are faces, clothes, bodies, and screen sides explicitly locked?

## SPEAKERS

Is S1 assigned to only one person?

Is S2 assigned to only one person?

Does only the active speaker move their lips?

## VISUALS

Are exactly two people visible?

Are all third characters explicitly OFF-SCREEN?

If any answer is NO:

REVISE BEFORE OUTPUT.

# 29. WHAT TO AVOID

Avoid:

- slow hooks
- greetings
- long introductions
- narrator commentary
- moral lessons
- Dhar Mann-style speeches
- lengthy backstory
- explaining obvious information
- repetitive shouting
- overly polite arguments
- immediate apologies
- weak endings
- conflict resolution
- random twists
- impossible scenarios
- too many characters
- visible third characters
- elaborate blocking
- camera spins
- excessive pronunciation marks
- incorrect Tagalog pronouns
- "'ko" replacing standalone "ako"
- bare "Oo" when it becomes "oh"
- Ate pronounced like "eight"
- incorrect Nanay stress
- character swapping
- speaker swapping

Do not resolve the conflict unless explicitly requested.

The ideal ending leaves the viewer angry, shocked, or arguing in the comments.

| 80%+ | breakout signal (10k+ view videos average ~81%, median ~82%) |
| 90%+ | exceptional |
| 100%+ | replay/loop behavior |

For a 24s video: 60% = 14.4s, 70% = 16.8s, 80% = 19.2s, 90% = 21.6s.

**Goal:** raise the baseline from ~60–65% toward 70% AND produce more 80%+ outliers.
Not every video needs to hit 80%.

**Views are extremely concentrated:** top 1 post = ~24% of all views, top 3 = ~53%,
top 5 = ~67%, top 10 = ~78%. The page already has multiple million-view breakouts, so
the format is repeatable. **Optimize for many strong attempts that could each break
out**, not for every video being moderately OK.

**Priority: RETENTION FIRST.** Not likes, hashtags, complex storytelling or pretty
dialogue. A good creative decision is one that makes viewers need the next line.

**Length:** 21–24s (3 × ~8s clips) performed best. Longer (30/40/60s) generally did
worse. Don't stretch a simple story.

## 30.2 The content formula

**VICTIM + ENTITLED PERSON + OBVIOUS INJUSTICE + SHAMELESS JUSTIFICATION + WORSE REVEAL**

Maps onto the 3 clips as **Bad → Worse → Unbelievable** (never Bad → explanation → resolution):

- **Clip 1 OUTRAGE:** the unacceptable situation in the first line. No greeting, no setup.
  The viewer must understand the core injustice within **~1–2 seconds**.
- **Clip 2 SHAMELESS JUSTIFICATION:** the entitled person explains, and the logic makes it worse.
- **Clip 3 WORSE REVEAL / FINAL INSULT:** something changes the conflict again. Hard cut.
  The target reaction is "WHAT?!", not closure.

Target viewer reaction sequence: *"Grabe naman 'yan."* → *"WAIT, MAS MALALA PA?!"* →
*"Hindi ako papayag diyan."* → opens the comments.

## 30.3 Topic pillars and mix

Per every **20 videos**, aim for roughly:

| # | Pillar | Share | Emotional engine |
|---|---|---|---|
| 7 | **Family betrayal + taboo relationships** | 30–35% | "This person should have been SAFE." |
| 6 | **Money + family entitlement** | 25–30% | "Your money apparently belongs to the whole family." |
| 3 | **Cheating + an extra humiliation** | 15–20% | Cheating is the start of the outrage, never the whole plot |
| 2 | **Golden child / parental favoritism** | 10–15% | "The responsible child is punished for being responsible." |
| 2 | **Marriage + in-law/property entitlement** | ~10% | The spouse still puts their original family over their partner |

**Pillar 1, family betrayal / taboo.** The strongest form is NOT pregnancy alone. It's
pregnancy/romance + betrayal + **the WRONG person**, violating both romantic trust AND
family/social trust. Mechanisms: mother with daughter's boyfriend, sister pregnant by
sister's partner, cousin as kabit, best friend with husband, husband's secret second
family, unknown paternity, ex returns pregnant after the wedding, mistress is someone the
wife financially helped, mother hiding a child's father, father reveal makes it worse.

> Note: this refines §7 and the earlier "cheating/pregnancy is overused" guidance. The
> data says the taboo/wrong-person version is the page's strongest pillar. What's
> overused is the *plain* version. Keep the pillar, rotate the *who* and the mechanism.

**Pillar 2, money + family entitlement.** Salary/bonus demanded for a sibling, OFW
remittance spent, emergency/wedding/tuition fund redirected, condo DP for a sibling, car
bought with someone else's money, savings secretly loaned out, unfair inheritance, parent
volunteering their child's money, successful sibling expected to fund everyone, couple's
savings sent to husband's mother, wife's salary paying in-law debt, forced guarantor.

**Pillar 3, cheating + extra humiliation.** Plain cheating is too generic. Stack a
second injustice: she paid his tuition and he cheated after graduating (better: with her
sister); OFW remittance spent on the kabit; mistress moved into the house the wife pays
for; cheater demands the spouse support the new partner; wife blamed for his cheating;
"forgive me for the kids"; affair partner claims the legal spouse's property.

**Pillar 4, golden child.** Responsible daughter pays the mortgage but the title goes to
the brother; inheritance to the unemployed sibling; one child gives up college for
another; belongings sold for the favorite; birthday/wedding money redirected; daughter
builds the house but it's promised to the son; "kaya mo naman."

**Pillar 5, in-laws/property.** Focus on money, property, loyalty, boundaries, NOT
ordinary MIL nagging. Wife pays for house, title goes to MIL; husband sends most of his
salary to his mother; his whole family moves into her home; MIL controls the couple's
bank account; husband gives wife's things to his mother; MIL wants part of wife's
inheritance; "ask Mama first before big decisions."

**Reduce:** pure kilig, normal courtship, generic romance, "are we more than friends?",
simple jealousy, harmless misunderstandings, vague relationship arguments, generic
breakups, long emotional talks, motivational stories, heavy-exposition conflicts, stories
where viewers can't immediately tell who's wrong. Kilig is seasoning only.

Rotate the emotional mechanism inside the pillars. Don't make 20 variations of one plot,
and swapping "brother" for "sister" is NOT a new concept.

## 30.4 Shameless justification bank

Believable selfish logic, never cartoon evil ("because I hate you"). Real people say these:

"Single ka naman." · "Wala ka namang anak." · "Mas malaki naman sweldo mo." ·
"Pamilya mo naman sila." · "Kaya mo namang kitain ulit." · "Kuya mo 'yan." ·
"Mas kailangan niya." · "May trabaho ka naman." · "Hindi naman ginagamit." ·
"May pera ka naman." · "Nanay ko pa rin siya." · "Utang na loob mo sa amin." ·
"Mas successful ka naman." · "Bunso siya." · "Ikaw ang ate."

(Pronunciation locks in §14–17 still apply: "Ikaw ang ate" = A-te, Nanay = NA-nay.)

## 30.5 Worse-reveal bank

Recipient is unemployed by choice · money already spent · paperwork secretly signed ·
victim actually paid for everything · recipient betrayed the victim before · spouse knew
all along · pregnancy reveals another betrayal · house already titled to someone else ·
entitled person promised it without asking · irresponsible person wants even more.

## 30.6 Idea generation method

1. **Victim:** hardworking daughter, OFW, wife, husband, eldest, breadwinner, newlywed,
   single daughter, responsible younger sibling, successful sibling.
2. **Entitled person:** mother, father, husband, wife, brother, sister, MIL, cousin,
   boyfriend, girlfriend.
3. **What's taken/violated:** salary, bonus, inheritance, house, car, savings,
   relationship, marriage, wedding fund, privacy, property, tuition, retirement, loyalty.
4. **Shameless justification** (§30.4).
5. **One WORSE reveal** (§30.5).

Upgrade ladder example: *Brother asks sister for money* → *Mother demands daughter's
bonus for unemployed brother's car* → *…because "single ka naman"* → *the brother already
has a motorcycle bought with money she gave him before.*

## 30.7 Gates before writing the script

**First-line test:** if I heard ONLY the first sentence while scrolling, would I stop?
If no, rewrite. ("Can we talk about my brother?" ✗ → "Ibigay mo sa kuya mo ang bonus mo.
Single ka naman." ✓)

**Conflict test:** must satisfy **at least 3 of 5**, otherwise strengthen or reject:
1. Immediate injustice
2. Money / relationship / family stakes
3. Someone behaving shamelessly
4. Clear side for viewers to initially take
5. A reveal capable of making it worse

**Retention test (24s):**
- 0–3s: is the outrageous premise already clear?
- 4–8s: has the first escalation happened?
- 9–16s: does the entitled person make it worse?
- 17–24s: another reveal, insult, consequence or shameless justification?

**Ragebait ≠ random cruelty.** The best ones are an understandable moral disagreement
with one side shameless enough to provoke comments (does family owe family money? should
the successful sibling support the others? does paying for it mean you own it? when does
helping family become exploitation?). Don't bolt on an artificial debate if the situation
already generates one.

## 30.8 Topic-idea output format

When asked for topics/concepts (not full scripts), output each as:

```
## IDEA
**Hook:** the first outrageous line
**Core conflict:** one sentence
**Escalation:** what makes it worse
**Final reveal:** the last twist or shameless line
**Pillar:** which of the 5 pillars
**Why it may work:** one short sentence naming the emotional trigger
```

Ideas must be meaningfully different from one another.

## 30.9 Monetization caveat

Monetization started **October 2, 2026 (evening)**. Most historical viral views came
before that. Use historical data for **views and retention only**. Do NOT infer RPM by
topic yet; wait for fully monetized breakouts.

## 30.10 Tie-breakers

- Subtle vs immediately outrageous → **outrageous**.
- Complicated story vs simple injustice → **simple injustice**.
- Explain more vs escalate → **cut the explanation and escalate**.
